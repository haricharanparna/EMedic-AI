# Common Illnesses

## Cold vs. flu vs. COVID-19
- **Colds** come on slowly with a runny or stuffy nose, sneezing and a mild sore throat. They usually get better in 7–10 days.
- **Flu** hits suddenly with fever, chills, body aches, headache and exhaustion.
- **COVID-19** can look like either one, sometimes with loss of taste or smell. A home test can tell the difference.
Flu antiviral medicine (like Tamiflu) works best when started **within 48 hours** of symptoms. It's especially important for people 65+, pregnant people, young children and people with chronic conditions.

## Fever in adults
A fever is a temperature of **100.4°F (38°C) or higher**. Rest, drink plenty of fluids, and use acetaminophen or ibuprofen as directed on the label if you're uncomfortable. Get medical care if your fever is **103°F (39.4°C) or higher**, lasts **more than 3 days**, or comes with a stiff neck, confusion, a new rash, trouble breathing or chest pain.

## Fever in babies and children
- **Under 3 months:** any fever of **100.4°F (38°C) or higher** needs care right away. Call the Nurse Line or go to the ER.
- **Older babies and children:** call your child's provider if a fever lasts **more than 24 hours in a child under 2**, or **more than 3 days** in an older child, or if it reaches **104°F (40°C)**.
- Get care right away if your child is very sleepy or hard to wake, has trouble breathing, won't drink, has far fewer wet diapers, or has a seizure.
Never give aspirin to children or teens.

## Sore throat and strep throat
Most sore throats are caused by viruses and get better on their own in about a week. Signs that point to **strep throat** (which needs antibiotics) are a sore throat **without a cough**, fever, swollen neck glands, and white patches on the tonsils. A quick strep test at a clinic or urgent care can confirm it. Get care right away if you have trouble swallowing saliva or breathing.

## Stomach bug and dehydration
For vomiting or diarrhea, take **small, frequent sips** of water or an oral rehydration drink, then add bland foods as you feel better. Get care if you **can't keep fluids down for 24 hours** (about 8 hours for young children), see blood in vomit or stool, have severe belly pain, or show signs of dehydration: very little urine, dizziness, dry mouth, or no tears in a child.

## Urinary tract infections (UTIs)
Common symptoms are burning when you pee, needing to go often or urgently, and cloudy or strong-smelling urine. UTIs usually need antibiotics, and simple UTIs can often be treated through a **video visit**. Get care the same day if you have **fever, back or side pain, vomiting**, blood in your urine, are pregnant, or are male. These can mean a more serious infection.

## Seasonal allergies
Sneezing, itchy eyes and a runny nose that come back in the same seasons are often allergies. Over-the-counter antihistamines (cetirizine, loratadine, fexofenadine) and steroid nasal sprays usually help. Keep windows closed on high-pollen days and shower after being outside. **Call 911** for signs of a severe reaction (anaphylaxis): swelling of the lips, tongue or throat, trouble breathing, or fainting. Use an epinephrine auto-injector if you have one.

## Headaches and migraines
Most headaches are tension headaches and improve with rest, water, food and over-the-counter pain relievers. Migraines often cause throbbing pain on one side, nausea, and sensitivity to light. **Get emergency care** for the worst headache of your life that comes on suddenly, a headache after a head injury, or a headache with fever and stiff neck, confusion, weakness, or vision loss. See your provider if you need pain relievers more than 2–3 days a week.
<!-- keywords: headache migraine head pain throbbing -->

## Back pain
Most back pain gets better within a few weeks. Staying gently active helps more than bed rest. Heat, stretching and over-the-counter pain relievers can ease it. **Get care right away** if back pain comes with numbness in the groin or inner thighs, loss of bladder or bowel control, leg weakness, fever, or follows a bad fall or accident.

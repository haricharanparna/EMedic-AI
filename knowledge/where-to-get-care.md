# Where to Get Care

## Emergency room vs. urgent care vs. video visit
- **Call 911 or go to the ER** for life-threatening problems: chest pain, trouble breathing, stroke signs, severe bleeding, major injuries, fainting, or sudden confusion.
- **Urgent care** is for problems that need care today but aren't life-threatening: sprains, minor cuts that need stitches, ear pain, high fever, vomiting. EMedic Urgent Care is open **8 AM–10 PM daily**.
- **Video visits** work well for colds, flu symptoms, pink eye, rashes, urinary infections, refills and mental health. Available **7 AM–11 PM daily**.
- **Your primary care provider** is best for checkups, ongoing conditions and anything that can wait a day or two.

## Heart attack warning signs
Call **911** right away if you notice: chest pain, pressure or squeezing that lasts more than a few minutes or comes back; pain spreading to the arm, jaw, neck or back; shortness of breath; cold sweat; nausea; or lightheadedness. Women, older adults and people with diabetes more often have shortness of breath, nausea or unusual tiredness instead of strong chest pain. Don't drive yourself. The 911 dispatcher may tell you to chew an aspirin.

## Stroke warning signs (BE FAST)
Use **BE FAST** and call **911** immediately if you see any of these:
- **B**alance: sudden loss of balance or coordination
- **E**yes: sudden vision loss or double vision
- **F**ace: one side of the face droops
- **A**rms: one arm is weak or drifts down
- **S**peech: slurred or confused speech
- **T**ime: call 911 and note when symptoms started
Stroke treatments work best within the first few hours, so every minute counts.

## Mental health crisis help
If you or someone else is thinking about suicide or is in emotional crisis, **call or text 988** (the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) any time, day or night. You can also chat at 988lifeline.org. If someone is in immediate danger, call **911**. EMedic Behavioral Health also offers same-week appointments and doesn't require a referral.

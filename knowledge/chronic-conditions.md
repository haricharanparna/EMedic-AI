# Chronic Conditions

## Blood pressure numbers
- **Normal:** below 120/80
- **Elevated:** 120–129 top number and below 80 bottom number
- **High blood pressure, stage 1:** 130–139 or 80–89
- **High blood pressure, stage 2:** 140 or higher, or 90 or higher
- **Severe:** above 180/120. Wait 5 minutes and recheck. If it's still that high, contact your provider right away. **Call 911** if you also have chest pain, shortness of breath, back pain, numbness, weakness, vision changes or trouble speaking.

## Lowering blood pressure
Lifestyle changes can lower blood pressure noticeably: cut back on salt (aim for under 2,300 mg of sodium a day), eat more fruits, vegetables and whole grains, get about **150 minutes of activity a week**, limit alcohol, don't smoke, and manage stress. If you take blood pressure medicine, take it every day, even when you feel fine. Home monitoring helps your provider fine-tune treatment.

## Diabetes and prediabetes numbers
- **A1C** (average blood sugar over 3 months): normal is below **5.7%**, prediabetes is **5.7–6.4%**, diabetes is **6.5% or higher**.
- **Fasting blood sugar:** normal is below **100 mg/dL**, prediabetes is **100–125**, diabetes is **126 or higher**.
Prediabetes can often be reversed with weight loss of 5–7% and regular activity. EMedic offers a free diabetes prevention program.

## Low blood sugar (hypoglycemia)
Low blood sugar is below **70 mg/dL**. Signs include shakiness, sweating, fast heartbeat, hunger, confusion and irritability. Use the **15-15 rule**: eat or drink **15 grams of fast sugar** (4 oz of juice or regular soda, or 3–4 glucose tablets), then recheck in **15 minutes**. Repeat if still low. Once it's normal, eat a snack. **Call 911** if the person passes out or can't safely swallow.

## Cholesterol numbers
A basic cholesterol test (lipid panel) measures total cholesterol, LDL ("bad"), HDL ("good") and triglycerides. Generally: **total under 200 mg/dL**, **LDL under 100**, **HDL 40 or higher** for men and **50 or higher** for women, and **triglycerides under 150**. Your provider looks at your overall heart risk, not just one number, to decide whether you need treatment.

## Asthma flare-ups
Use your quick-relief (rescue) inhaler as your asthma action plan directs. **Get emergency care** if your rescue inhaler isn't helping, you're too short of breath to speak in full sentences, your lips or fingernails look blue or gray, or the skin between your ribs pulls in with each breath. If you need your rescue inhaler more than twice a week, talk to your provider about better control.
